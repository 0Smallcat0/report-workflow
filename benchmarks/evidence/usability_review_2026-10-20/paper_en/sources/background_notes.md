# Background notes

Survey method: 40 first- and second-year undergraduates in an introductory statistics course self-reported average nightly sleep over the two weeks before the midterm and weekly study hours. Midterm scores (0-100) were taken from the course gradebook with consent. The survey ran from 3 to 14 March 2026.

Walker (2017, Why We Sleep) argues that sleep after learning consolidates memory.

Okano et al. (2019, npj Science of Learning) found that sleep quality and duration over the month before exams explained about 25% of the variance in grades of 88 MIT students.

Limitations noted by the instructor: sleep was self-reported, the sample is one course at one university, and students who slept more may differ in other ways (for example, fewer part-time job hours).
